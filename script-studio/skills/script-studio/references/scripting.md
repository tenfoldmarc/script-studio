# Scripting: short-form scripts that hold attention

For Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts, 20 to 60 seconds. One core idea per script. If the idea has two, script the stronger one and offer the other as its own idea.

Before writing, read: the idea card, `<state>/voice.md` (house structure, words per second, CTA style, proof bank), `<state>/taste.md`, and `hooks.md`. Every script runs the VOICE GATE in `voice.md` before it is shown.

---

## Before you write a word

Answer these in a line each. If you can't, the script isn't ready.

1. **The withheld payoff.** The single answer, twist, or result the viewer stays for. It resolves no earlier than about 60% of the way through.
2. **The tension.** What the viewer wants vs what's in the way, in the audience's own words.
3. **The shape.** Story or structured list (see story shapes below). Default to the buyer's house structure.
4. **The length.** Pick from the pacing table. Say as much as the idea has, no more.
5. **The CTA**, or none.

---

## The structure, beat by beat

| Beat | Job | Share of runtime |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Open the main loop. Mid-thought, mid-story, or a callout. Never a greeting. | 0 to 8% |
| Re-hook / stakes | Why this matters to them right now: the cost of not knowing, or a quick credibility line. Sharpens the promise without answering it. | 8 to 20% |
| Body | 2 to 4 stages, one or two sentences each. Each stage opens a small loop or pays off a small one and opens the next. | 20 to 60% |
| The turn | A "but" or a beat shift around 40%: a surprise, a failed attempt, a counterexample, a new thread. This is where viewers drop, so something has to change. | around 40% |
| Payoff | Resolve the main loop. Concrete. The line they'd screenshot or repeat to a friend. | 60 to 85% |
| CTA | One ask, in their words, flowing from the payoff. | last 10 to 15% |

Timing in seconds:

| Length | Hook | Re-hook | Body (turn at) | Payoff | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20s | 0 to 2 | 2 to 4 | 4 to 12 (8) | 12 to 18 | 18 to 20 |
| 30s | 0 to 3 | 3 to 6 | 6 to 18 (12) | 18 to 26 | 26 to 30 |
| 45s | 0 to 3 | 3 to 8 | 8 to 27 (18) | 27 to 40 | 40 to 45 |
| 60s | 0 to 3 | 3 to 9 | 9 to 36 (24) | 36 to 54 | 54 to 60 |

The bones should be invisible. If a viewer can feel "now here's the problem, now here's the solution", rewrite until it sounds like a person telling a friend something.

## Story-first shapes

Education lands harder wrapped in a story or a secret. Pick one:

- **Two paths.** "There are two ways people handle a lowball offer." Path one in full, path two held back until the payoff.
- **Cold open with a punchline.** Drop into the middle of a scene. Everything builds to one line at the end. Example: a plumber's worst call, ending on what was actually clogging the drain.
- **Question into story.** "Do you know why nurses never sit on hospital beds? No?" Then the story that answers it.
- **Insider reveal.** "Here's how pricing at a car dealership actually works." The viewer feels like they're overhearing something.
- **Before and after.** The ugly before in specific detail, the one change, the after. Works for kitchens, budgets, bodies, inboxes.
- **Mistake story.** "The $6K mistake I made on my first wedding cake." The lesson comes out of the cost.
- **Experiment log.** "I answered every lead in under 5 minutes for 30 days." Day markers create natural mini-loops.
- **List with scenes.** A numbered list where every item is a tiny scene, not a bullet. Reveal items one at a time.

Use copywriting frameworks (problem, agitate, solve and the rest) only as invisible scaffolding.

---

## Retention mechanics

- **Nested loops.** The main loop holds until 60%+. Inside it, stack mini-loops that each buy 5 to 10 seconds.
- **Pop and rebuild.** The sentence right after any payoff opens a new question. Otherwise they leave.
- **"But" cliffhangers.** At least 1, ideally 2. "But that's not what killed the deal." Each one adds new tension.
- **Fight the 40% valley.** Put the turn there. Never a recap or a slow explanation at 40%.
- **Pacing is information speed, not talking speed.** One or two sentences per pocket of information, then move.
- **Cut 20% on the second pass.** Almost every first draft has it. If a sentence needs "for example" to defend itself, cut it.
- **Nuance goes in the caption.** Hedges and "this depends on your situation" kill pace in the script. Put them in the caption.
- **Specific beats vague.** A number, a timeframe, a named situation, a visible object. "Better results" means nothing. "Two extra showings a week" means something.
- **Viewer-facing by default.** Lead with the viewer's world. Use the buyer's personal story when it genuinely fits, not in every script.
- **Change the picture every 2 to 4 seconds** in the visual notes: cut, zoom, B-roll, text change, object in hand.
- **No manipulative teases.** "Stay till the end" increases drop-off. The loop does that job.
- **Payoff before the CTA.** Never make them sit through the ask to get the answer.

## Pacing in words per second

Spoken pace is about 2.5 to 3 words per second. If `voice.md` has the buyer's measured words per second, use theirs: target words = seconds x their rate x 0.9. Write to the low end. Real delivery adds pauses, emphasis, and reactions.

| Length | Target words (2.5 to 3 w/s) | Hook (first sentence) |
|---|---|---|
| 20s | 50 to 60 | 8 to 12 words |
| 30s | 75 to 90 | 8 to 12 words |
| 45s | 110 to 135 | 8 to 12 words |
| 60s | 150 to 180 | 8 to 12 words |

Count the spoken words before showing the script. Report the count next to the length target.

## Use the buyer's best structure as the template

The buyer's own top reels (by multiplier) show the shape their audience already rewards.

1. Take the `House structure` section in `voice.md`. If it's missing, map their top 3 reels now: hook mechanism, seconds to first payoff, number of stages, story or list, where the CTA sits, total length and word count.
2. If 2 of the 3 share a shape, that is the default template. Pour the new idea into it.
3. Break from it only when the idea needs a different shape (a two-path story doesn't fit a list template). Say so in one line above the script.
4. If the source outlier had a strong shape of its own, blend 70/30: the source's shape, the buyer's rhythm, length, and CTA habit.

## CTA types

One CTA per reel. Use the buyer's CTA wording from `voice.md`. Not every reel needs a hard ask.

| CTA | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Comment keyword | They have something real to send (guide, checklist, template) and ideally an auto-DM set up. Middle or bottom of funnel. | "Comment SHELF and I'll send you the exact post I use." |
| Follow | A series ("part 2 tomorrow") or a high-value reel that earned it. Never in the first seconds. | "I'm doing all five of these this week. Follow so you don't miss part 2." |
| Save | Dense, reusable content: steps, recipes, checklists, scripts to say. | "Save this for your next open house." |
| Share / send | Broad, relatable reels. Name who to send it to. | "Send this to the friend who still pays for every repair." |
| DM | High-ticket offers, warm audiences, bottom of funnel. | "DM me 'audit' and I'll look at yours." |
| None | Pure reach reels. Let the payoff land and end. | |

If the buyer has no lead magnet or keyword set up, don't promise one. Use `[VERIFY: what you'll send when they comment]` or pick a different CTA.

When the reel's main goal is conversion, the CTA can also go in the caption's first line. Most people read the caption and far fewer finish the video.

## The film-it note

Always tell them how to shoot it:

- **One take** for stories, rants, and reactions. Energy and personality carry them, and a few stumbles feel real.
- **Line by line** for dense lists, numbers, or long lines. Each line becomes a clean cut, which also gives the editor more to work with.
- **Camera.** Eye level or slightly above. The back camera beats the front camera whenever they don't need to see themselves. Tripod or propped for talking head, handheld for walk-and-talks and demos.
- **Framing.** Chest up, face in the upper middle of the frame so on-screen text and captions sit clear of it.
- **A second angle** (turn 90 degrees, or move from desk to kitchen) gives a fresh look if they film a variation for a second post.
- **The script is training wheels.** Once they know the beats, suggest recording from the bracketed beat labels alone so their personality survives.

## QC checklist

Every box passes before the script is shown:

- [ ] One core idea, one withheld payoff.
- [ ] Hook passes the open-loop test. No greeting, no "in this video".
- [ ] Spoken hook, on-screen text, and caption first line each hit a different angle. None repeats another.
- [ ] On-screen hook is 6 to 9 words on two lines max.
- [ ] Main loop resolves at 60% or later. Nothing in the first 15 seconds accidentally answers the hook.
- [ ] At least one "but" or beat shift, and something changes around 40%.
- [ ] Every payoff is followed by a new open question, until the final payoff.
- [ ] Word count fits the length target at the buyer's pace.
- [ ] Specifics present: numbers, situations, objects. No abstract metaphors.
- [ ] Every unproven fact is a `[VERIFY: ...]` slot, listed under "Before you film".
- [ ] No borrowed stories turned into the buyer's first-person claims.
- [ ] Exactly one CTA (or a deliberate none), in their wording, after the payoff.
- [ ] Visual notes change the picture every few seconds and frame one works on mute.
- [ ] Caption first line complements the hook, nuance lives in the caption, 3 to 5 hashtags.
- [ ] Three alternate hooks, each with all three surfaces.
- [ ] Nothing breaks an `avoid` lesson in `taste.md`.
- [ ] VOICE GATE passed and the gate report is attached.
