# Hooks: craft, templatize, and write options

The first 1 to 3 seconds decide whether anything else gets watched. But the hook rides on the idea: if a strong hook won't come within a few minutes, the idea is weak. Go back to `ideas.md` instead of polishing a hook for a flat idea.

Read the buyer's `voice.md` (openers, hook types they lean on) and `<state>/hook-bank.md` before writing hooks.

---

## The four surfaces

Every reel hooks on four surfaces at the same moment. Each one does a different job.

| Surface | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken first line | The tension in words. Usually the clearest surface. | First word inside the first second. First sentence done in about 3 seconds (8 to 12 words). Whole hook beat done by 8 seconds. |
| On-screen text | The topic label plus a curiosity pull. Carries the 30%+ who watch on mute. | 6 to 9 words, hard cap 9. Two lines, never three. Optional 2 to 4 word parenthetical "whisper" underneath for proof, risk reversal, or a named method. Holds 7 to 10 seconds. |
| Caption first line | Whatever the other two didn't cover: proof, stakes, a question, or the CTA if the goal is conversion. | Fits before the "more" cut (about 100 to 125 characters). Never a summary of the video. |
| Frame one visual | What a muted scroller sees before any word lands. | Must make sense with no sound. Best when something is about to happen: a pour, a reveal, a before shot, an object in hand. |

### The complement rule

- **Never repeat a surface verbatim on another surface.** Reading and hearing the same words wastes the second channel. This is the most common hook mistake.
- **Lean opposite directions.** If on-screen leans curiosity, spoken leans clarity, and the reverse.
- **Together they preview the whole video** in the first 3 to 5 seconds, not one 10 second slice of it.
- **Sound-off test.** Frame one plus on-screen text alone must tell a muted viewer what this is and why to stay.

Example, a real estate agent reel about pricing a home:

- Frame one: agent holding a "SOLD" sign upside down in front of a house.
- On-screen: "Why overpriced homes sell for LESS (the 21-day rule)"
- Spoken: "Every seller I talk to wants to list high and come down later. Here's what that actually costs you."
- Caption first line: "Pulled this from [VERIFY: number] of my own listings. The day 21 number surprised me too."

Four surfaces, four angles, one video.

## Open loop vs declarative

**The test:** after the hook, does the viewer already know your take? If yes, rewrite it. Imply the take. Sell the mystery of the conclusion, not the conclusion.

| Declarative (closes the loop) | Open loop (take withheld) |
|---|---|
| "You should stop eating oatmeal for breakfast." | "The breakfast that's quietly making you hungrier by 11am." |
| "Always get a second quote on roof repairs." | "The one question that made a roofer drop his quote by a third." |
| "Your credit score matters more than your income." | "The number banks check before they even look at your salary." |
| "Consistency is the key to growing a bakery." | "We sold out every Saturday after we changed one thing about our menu board." |

Statement-shaped hooks are fine when the take stays hidden ("I lost $80K on my first flip" withholds the how). The rule is the test, not the punctuation.

## Hook mechanisms

Name the mechanism for every hook you write or bank. Stack 2 or 3 at most. The reliable default stack: a specific pain, the dream outcome, the curiosity gap between them, with proof in the caption.

| # | Mechanism | One-line example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Curiosity gap | "There's a setting on your oven that most people have never touched." |
| 2 | Contrarian | "I tell every new client to stop doing cardio." |
| 3 | Pain callout (twist the knife) | "If you've rewritten your resume four times and still get no callbacks, it's not the resume." |
| 4 | Identity callout | "If you own a salon and you still book by text, watch this." |
| 5 | Identity contrast | "Broke landlords fix the kitchen. Rich landlords fix the lease." |
| 6 | Pain to dream bridge | "From $0 saved to a full emergency fund on a nurse's salary." |
| 7 | Proof-first result | "This one email got my cleaning company 11 new clients in a week." |
| 8 | Mistake or warning | "The protein mistake I see in almost every woman over 40." |
| 9 | Insider reveal | "Car dealers have a word for buyers like you." |
| 10 | Named concept | "It's called the empty plate rule, and it fixed my picky eater." |
| 11 | Story cold open | "My first restaurant review was one star, and the reviewer was my mom." |
| 12 | Question they can't answer | "Do you know why hotel pillows feel different from yours? No?" |
| 13 | Versus or ranking | "I ranked every budget app by how fast it made me quit." |
| 14 | List with a hook item | "3 things I'd never buy at Costco, and one is the hot dog." |
| 15 | Experiment or challenge | "I posted my listing at 6am for 30 days. Here's what moved." |
| 16 | Price anchor | "Your contractor charges $400 for this. It's a $12 part." |
| 17 | Speed or time box | "Fix a squeaky door in 20 seconds with something in your pantry." |
| 18 | Risk reversal | "No gym, no equipment, and it takes less time than your coffee." |
| 19 | Pump fake | "Everyone thinks the dough is why their pizza is soggy. It's the oven tray." |
| 20 | Visual anticipation | Tape measure stretched across a tiny bathroom, before a word is said. |
| 21 | Timely bridge | "Rates just moved again. Here's the only number first-time buyers should watch." |
| 22 | Reaction or stitch | "A dentist reacts to the viral teeth-whitening trick." The clip must explain itself with no setup (evidence without context). |

Rules that apply across all of them:

- **Most important word first.** Filler at the front costs the 1 to 2 second cliff.
- **Fifth-grade words.** Any term that needs explaining loses the viewer.
- **Informal beats formal.** Text-to-a-friend tone wins.
- **"How" and "what" beat "why"** for growth hooks. People want the move, not the philosophy.
- **Nouns over verbs on screen.** Capitalize the one meaningful noun, not filler words. Keep function words (is, the, your, if, but) under a third of the overlay.
- **Specific beats vague.** "Things", "better", "success", "more" get replaced with a number, a timeframe, a named situation.
- **Earn superlatives.** "Biggest", "best", "number one", "worst" only with proof attached.
- **Hook window by size.** Accounts under about 1K followers get about 1.5 seconds. Bigger accounts earn a little more. Write for the short window either way.

### Anti-patterns (rewrite on sight)

- "In this video", "Hey guys, welcome back", "Hi, I'm <name> and I'm a <job>" as the first line.
- "Stay till the end", "the last one will shock you", "you won't believe".
- On-screen text that repeats the spoken line.
- Answering the hook in the first 10 seconds.
- Bait the body doesn't pay off. The hook is a promise. Break it and they won't trust the next one.
- Climax-first hooks with only words. If it needs a visual payoff to make sense and there isn't one, it just confuses. Confused viewers scroll.
- Generic listicle items shown on screen. If the items aren't curious on their own, reveal them one at a time.
- Hooks about the creator when the viewer has no reason to care yet ("So I've been thinking a lot lately...").
- Fake scarcity or fake proof. Never.
- Over-engineered hooks that feel like a template. If it sounds like a swipe file, the audience bounces.

## Templatize a competitor's outlier hook

Only bank hooks from posts that qualify as outliers (see `ideas.md`: 3x or more against that creator's own median, not a tiny account, at least 3 days old, never marked trial or repeat). Raw views alone never qualify.

**Fluke test for 8x or more.** Before learning from it, check: the brief marks it `[collab or paid]` (weigh it lightly), the comments are mostly hostile or off-topic (a paid push), frame one is an ad or product branding, or the creator has no other reel above 3x (a one-off). A fluke still counts as evidence of the topic, not of the hook: don't bank its hook.

1. **Capture all four surfaces.** Spoken (first 1 to 3 sentences of the transcript), on-screen (read it off the first-frame screenshot), caption first line, and one line describing frame one.
2. **Find the surface that carried it.** Usually one does most of the work. Note it.
3. **Name the mechanism(s)** from the table above.
4. **Explain why it worked in one sentence:** the lever plus who it hit. "Price anchor against a pro the viewer already resents paying." If you can't explain it, don't bank it.
5. **Slot the specifics.** Swap topic nouns, numbers, timeframes, identities, and objects for descriptive `[slots]`. Keep the structure words, the rhythm, and the length within 2 words.
6. **Two-niche test.** Fill the template for two unrelated niches. If both read naturally, bank it. If it only works for the original topic, the power was the topic: save it as an idea source, not a hook template.

Worked example:

- Original spoken line (a meal-prep creator, 5.2x): "I stopped buying chicken breast and my grocery bill dropped by a third."
- Surface: spoken. Frame one was a receipt held to the camera.
- Mechanism: Contrarian + Proof-first result.
- Why: drops a default habit everyone has and attaches a money result they can feel.
- Template: `I stopped [default habit] and my [painful cost] dropped by [specific amount].`
- Test 1, salon owner: "I stopped taking walk-ins and my no-show rate dropped by half."
- Test 2, landlord: "I stopped answering tenant calls and my repair costs dropped by a third."
- Passes. Bank it. When used, the real numbers become `[VERIFY: ...]` unless they're in the buyer's proof bank.

### Hook bank file format

File: `<state>/hook-bank.md`. Append only. Never delete or rewrite an entry. Dedupe by source URL. Start a new file with these two lines:

```
# Hook bank: @<handle>
Format: template | mechanism @surface | original line | source URL | views and multiplier | creator, then an indented why: line
```

Then one entry per line, exactly six fields separated by ` | `, then an indented `why:` line:

```
template | mechanism @surface | original line | source URL | views and multiplier | creator
  why: <one sentence>
```

Example entries:

```
I stopped [default habit] and my [painful cost] dropped by [amount]. | Contrarian + Proof-first @spoken | I stopped buying chicken breast and my grocery bill dropped by a third. | https://www.instagram.com/reel/XXXX/ | 412K, 5.2x | @mealprepcreator
  why: drops a habit everyone has and pins a money result they can feel.
The [number]-day rule [identity] never break (when [situation]) | Named concept @on-screen | The 2-day rule trainers never break (when you're sore) | https://www.instagram.com/reel/YYYY/ | 96K, 3.8x | @coachcreator
  why: a named rule sounds like insider knowledge and the whisper pre-qualifies the viewer.
```

Rules for the bank:
- The buyer's own winners (1.5x or more of their own median, settled) go in too, with `self` as the creator. They are the strongest entries.
- Sort by multiplier when pulling, not by views.
- Reuse cap: the same template at most once per batch and not again for about 7 days. Tweak it slightly each time.

## Write hook options

This happens once the topic is agreed and BEFORE the script: the buyer picks the hook, then you write the script around it.

1. **Write the core tension in one sentence** before any hook: what the viewer wants, what's in the way, and the payoff the script withholds.
2. **Pull candidates.** The buyer's own top openers from `voice.md` first, then hook-bank templates sorted by multiplier, spread across mechanisms.
3. **How many:** 10 when the hook bank plus their own winning openers give you 10 strong, different options for this topic; fewer (never under 5) while the library is small. Never pad with weak ones. Mix:
   - At least 4 different mechanisms (6 when you write 10).
   - At least 2 built on the buyer's own proven hook patterns, when they have any.
   - The rest from bank templates, each citing its source.
   - At most 1 wildcard that isn't sourced.
4. **Every option has three lines:** spoken, on-screen, caption first line. The top 3 also get a frame one note.
5. **Gate each option:** open-loop test, complement rule, length rules, voice gate quick pass (`voice.md` Part 2), and "does the body pay this off?" Fix or drop any that fail.
6. **Rank.** Proven signal first (own winners, then source multiplier), then fit to this script's payoff, then voice match, then freshness against the last 10 posts and `taste.md`.
7. **Show them all, ranked, and let the buyer pick.** In chat, one numbered entry per option: the spoken line, the on-screen text, and one short line on why (its proof). Recommend your top pick in one line. They pick, combine two or reword one. Then write the script around their hook. In the saved script file, their hook is in the script and every other option goes under "Other hooks" in the format below.

Option format:

```
1. [Proof-first + Price anchor] from @source (4.1x, <URL>)  or  [your pattern, reel <N>]
   Spoken:    "<line>"
   On-screen: "<6 to 9 words>" (<optional whisper>)
   Caption:   "<first line>"
   Frame one: <visual, top 3 only>
   Why here:  <one sentence>
```

Example option, for a house cleaning business owner:

```
2. [Insider reveal + Identity callout] from bank template (3.6x, <URL>)
   Spoken:    "Cleaners can tell exactly what kind of client you are in the first five minutes."
   On-screen: "What your cleaner notices FIRST (be honest)"
   Caption:   "Asked my whole team [VERIFY: team size]. Number two was unanimous."
   Frame one: Cleaner pausing at a front door, eyebrows up, looking down at shoes by the mat.
   Why here:  Viewers can't resist finding out how they're judged, and it seeds the trust angle the body pays off.
```

If the buyer swaps in another option or edits the hook, log it in `taste.md` as a `(buyer)` line (see `ideas.md`).
